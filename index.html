A field guide · Redhill Close, Singapore

Ten plates of nasi lemak worth leaving home for.

A dedicationMade specially for the three craftlets — may every one of these plates be worth the drive.

Ten nasi lemak plates in Singapore's west and centre, every one of them within fifteen minutes of Redhill Close and — the part that took the most checking — actually open at the weekend. Ranked with a thumb on the scale for somewhere indoor to sit and a chicken curry worth ordering. Measured throughout against one reference plate, Cafe Sunset Railway.

◆The reference plate

Cafe Sunset Railway

41 Sunset Way · #01-02 Clementi Arcade · Thu–Sun only

Coconut basmati rice, chicken curry on the menu, a plate that arrives on ceramic.
Indoor cafe Chicken curry ✓ Sat–Sun 8am ✓

The counterpoint on this list — not a hawker stall but a small, quiet neighbourhood cafe that has become the yardstick for a certain kind of considered, sit-down nasi lemak. Basmati grain, chicken curry listed alongside the rice, and a room you can sit in out of the heat. It happens to satisfy both of the newer preferences, which is part of why it works as a reference. Every other entry is measured against it.

Bibik Violet

Temasek Shophouse · 28 Orchard Road · #01-05

Chicken curry at $14.90, in a 54-seat air-conditioned room, seven days a week.
Indoor · aircon Chicken curry ✓ Sat–Sun 10am

Violet Oon’s all-day Peranakan cafe in a restored heritage shophouse. It remains the only entry that satisfies every preference at once — indoor seating, a chicken curry listed in its own right, and open both weekend days. Weekday breakfast starts at 8am; on Saturday and Sunday the doors open at 10am instead, so it is a late brunch rather than an early one.

vs. Sunset RailwayThe same pairing — nasi lemak and chicken curry in a cafe setting — but open all seven days against the benchmark’s four. Sunset Railway answers back on portion size and on being a quieter room.

02Ann Siang Hill

The Coconut Club

Ann Siang Hill · shophouse dining room

Bario rice, ayam goreng berempah, and a rendang to order alongside.
Indoor · aircon Rendang, not curry chicken Open daily

The reference point for gourmet nasi lemak in Singapore, and a genuinely comfortable room to sit in — open every day including both weekend days. The caveat for your brief stands: the signature chicken here is fried berempah, not curried, and the curry on the menu is beef rendang.

vs. Sunset RailwayThe same idea with a bigger budget — both sit-down, ceramic-plate nasi lemak, but Coconut Club sources Bario coconuts from Sarawak and charges accordingly. Sunset Railway is the version you can justify weekly, and the one that actually serves chicken curry.

Straits Club Nasi Lemak

122 Bukit Merah Lane 1 · #01-50 · closed Fridays

Boneless berempah chicken with a poached egg — a tidier plate than the postcode suggests.
Indoor shop unit Curry unconfirmed Sat 10–7 · Sun 10–1

Five minutes from home and pitched between hawker and cafe: the signature boneless berempah set runs about $9.90 and arrives assembled rather than heaped. A proper shop unit, so you eat out of the heat. Both weekend days are covered, but note Sunday service stops at 1pm — and the stall is closed Fridays.

04Telok Ayer

Uptown Nasi Lemak

121 Telok Ayer Street · #01-01 Stall 7

A Malaysian-style plate in the middle of town, open every day of the week.
Sheltered food hall Curry unconfirmed Daily 10–14:30

One of the few genuinely reliable weekend options in the CBD, in a district where most hawker stalls shut on Saturday afternoon. Malaysian-style nasi lemak, served daily from 10am to 2:30pm. Two caveats worth knowing before you drive: it closes on public holidays, and on the last Saturday of every month. There is a second outlet at City Gate on Beach Road, open 11am to 8pm daily, if the timing suits better.

vs. Sunset RailwayCovers the days Sunset Railway does not, at a fraction of the price — but it is a lunch counter rather than a room, and there is no lingering over kopi afterwards.

Faith Nasi Lemak

Redhill Food Centre · 85 Redhill Lane · #01-30

Crispy wings and fried bee hoon — the breakfast stall on your own doorstep.
Open-air hawker Fried, not curried Seven days

The nearest entry on the list by a wide margin and the only one open all seven days without qualification: 6am to 12:30pm and again 5:30 to 8:30pm Monday through Saturday, and 6am to 2pm on Sunday. The crispy chicken wing is what the queue is for. If you want nasi lemak on a Sunday morning without planning anything, this is the answer.

vs. Sunset RailwayNo contest on convenience — two minutes away, a quarter of the price, and open every single day including the three Sunset Railway is shut. What it cannot give you is a table indoors or a plate that arrives on ceramic.

Tiong Bahru Market & Food Centre · #02-38 · halal

An early, halal-certified plate in Singapore’s prettiest market building.
Open-air hawker Curry unconfirmed Tue–Sun 7am–1pm

Five minutes up the road inside the Tiong Bahru Market, which is at its best on a weekend morning when nearly every stall is trading. Halal-certified, open Tuesday through Sunday from 7am, and closed Mondays. Come before nine and pair it with a coffee downstairs.

Nusa & Tara

ABC Brickworks Market & Food Centre · #01-22

Lontong and nasi lemak from a stall that opens at half past five in the morning.
Open-air hawker Fried, not curried From 5:30am

The Malay stall at ABC Brickworks, four minutes away, trading from 5:30am to 1:30pm. Well-executed classics — the lontong is what Miss Tam Chiak went for, but the nasi lemak is the better-value plate. Get there before the rice runs out, which on a weekend can be well before noon.

A cult couple-run plate with unusual toppings, from about $5.

A small, well-loved operation that has moved twice and kept its following each time, now settled on Cantonment Road. Unusual add-ons and a plate that starts around $5. Selera Rasa Nasi Lemak

Adam Road Food Centre · Stall 01-02 · closed Fridays

Patronised by the Sultan of Brunei; the fried chicken is the argument.
Open-air hawker Fried, not curried Sat–Sun 7am

The most-decorated Malay nasi lemak stall in Singapore, open 7am to 3pm on both Saturday and Sunday and closed on Fridays. The queue reflects the reputation — join it before 8:30 or plan for forty minutes. Crisp-skinned chicken, sambal with real depth, and rice you can taste the pandan in.

How the list was made

Every entry had to clear four bars: somewhere in Singapore's west or centre, a drive of fifteen minutes or less from Redhill Close, credible editorial coverage, and — checked stall by stall — confirmed trading hours on a Saturday or Sunday.

That last test removed more places than any other. Amoy Street Food Centre and Hong Lim are CBD hawker centres built for the office lunch crowd; most of their stalls shut from Saturday afternoon through Sunday, which cost this list two previous entries. Bukit Merah View, Hoyeah and Hjh Maimunah all close Sundays too. The far west went earlier, on drive time.

Ranking then favours an indoor room and a chicken curry actually on the menu. Where a stall's curry could not be confirmed from published sources, the card says so rather than guessing.

Weekend at a glance

Open both days: Bibik Violet, The Coconut Club, Uptown, Faith, Super Shiok, Nusa & Tara, Fong Seng, Selera Rasa. Saturday only: Mr & Mrs Nasi Lemak. Short Sunday: Straits Club closes at 1pm.

Three carry a standing caveat: Selera Rasa and Straits Club close on Fridays, and Uptown shuts on public holidays and the last Saturday of each month. Super Shiok is closed Mondays.

Verify before you drive

Hours here were confirmed against published listings in July 2026, but hawker stalls change days without notice and go on home leave. Every card links to a live Google Maps search — check it the morning you go. Redhill Close anchors the drive estimates.
