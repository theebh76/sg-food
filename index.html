A field guide · Redhill Close, Singapore

Ten plates of nasi lemak worth leaving home for.

A dedicationMade specially for the three craftlets — may every one of these plates be worth the drive.

A shortlist of coconut-rice breakfasts in Singapore's west and centre, all within fifteen minutes of Redhill Close. Ranked with a thumb on the scale for somewhere indoor to sit and a chicken curry worth ordering, then for breakfast hours and Google rating. Every entry is measured against a single reference plate — Cafe Sunset Railway.

◆The reference plate

Cafe Sunset Railway

41 Sunset Way · #01-02 Clementi Arcade · Thu–Sun only

Coconut basmati rice, chicken curry on the menu, a plate that arrives on ceramic.
Indoor cafe Chicken curry ✓ Thu–Sun · 8am

The counterpoint on this list — not a hawker stall but a small, quiet neighbourhood cafe that has become the yardstick for a certain kind of considered, sit-down nasi lemak. Basmati grain, chicken curry listed alongside the rice, and a room you can sit in out of the heat. It happens to satisfy both of the newer preferences, which is part of why it works as a reference. Every other entry is measured against it.

01Dhoby Ghaut

Bibik Violet

Temasek Shophouse · 28 Orchard Road · #01-05

Chicken curry on the menu at $14.90, in a 54-seat air-conditioned room.
Indoor · aircon Chicken curry ✓ Breakfast 8am

Violet Oon’s all-day Peranakan cafe in a restored heritage shophouse, open since September 2025. It is the only entry that satisfies every preference at once: proper indoor seating, a chicken curry listed on the menu in its own right, and nasi lemak available from 8am on a weekday. Order the curry alongside the nasi lemak rather than the ayam berempah — reviewers split on whether the fried chicken runs dry, but nobody complains about the curry.

vs. Sunset RailwayThe same pairing — nasi lemak and chicken curry in a cafe setting — but five weekday mornings instead of none, and a room built for sitting in. Sunset Railway answers back on portion size and on being ten minutes closer to a quiet corner of the west.

02Ann Siang Hill

The Coconut Club

Ann Siang Hill · shophouse dining room

Bario rice, ayam goreng berempah, and a rendang curry to order alongside.
Indoor · aircon Rendang, not curry chicken Opens 11am

The reference point for gourmet nasi lemak in Singapore, and a genuinely comfortable room to sit in. The caveat for your brief: the signature chicken here is fried berempah, not curried — the curry on the menu is beef rendang. Excellent, but a different dish from the one you’re after.

vs. Sunset RailwayThe same idea with a bigger budget — both sit-down, ceramic-plate nasi lemak, but Coconut Club sources Bario coconuts from Sarawak and charges accordingly. Sunset Railway is the version you can justify on a Thursday, and the one that actually serves chicken curry.

Patronised by the Sultan of Brunei; the fried chicken is the argument.

The most-decorated Malay nasi lemak stall in Singapore, and the queue reflects it — join it before 8:30 or plan for forty minutes. How the list was made

Every entry passed four tests: located in Singapore's west or central regions, a Google Maps rating at or near 4.5 stars, editorial coverage from at least one of SETHLUI, Eatbook, Miss Tam Chiak, Daniel Food Diary or a comparable guide, and a drive estimate of fifteen minutes or under from Redhill Close on a clear weekday morning.

Ranking then favours two things above the rest: an indoor room to eat in, and a chicken curry actually on the menu. Only three places clear both bars — Bibik Violet, the benchmark itself, and, with a caveat about rendang, The Coconut Club. Where a stall's curry could not be confirmed from published menus, it says so on the card rather than guessing.

East-side stalls were excluded by design — Geylang Serai, Old Airport Road and Tanjong Katong all have strong contenders, but none of them are a reasonable morning drive from Redhill. The far west went the same way: Boon Lay and Jurong West are past the twenty-minute line once the AYE is awake, whatever the map claims at 6am.

Preference was given to stalls open by 8am. Three entries — The Coconut Club (opens 11), Nasi Lemak Chinatown (10:30) and Straits Club (11) — earned their spot on quality and proximity alone. Nothing on this list is further than fifteen minutes out.

About the benchmark

Cafe Sunset Railway (41 Sunset Way, Clementi Arcade) is the reference plate rather than the winner. It sits at a useful midpoint — a sit-down cafe serving coconut basmati rice with rendang chicken, open 8am but only Thursday to Sunday — which makes it a fair yardstick for both the hawker stalls below it on price and the restaurants above it on ambition.

On the food alone, Selera Rasa beats it outright and Bibik Violet matches it on a weekday morning. On the experience — a table, a ceramic plate, and no queue — it holds its own against everything on this list.
